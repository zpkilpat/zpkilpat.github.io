Consulting & professional education

Mathematical modeling, decision science, and data science instruction for research groups, companies, and public-sector organizations.

AREAS OF EXPERTISE

Modeling, inference, and instruction

My consulting draws on our group’s research in Bayesian decision making, nonlinear dynamics, and stochastic processes. The models we build are mechanistic rather than black-box: their parameters stand for processes the system actually carries out, so a fit tells you why the data look the way they do, not only what comes next. We have fit Bayesian and heuristic models of human decision making to cohorts of hundreds of subjects, and decentralized models of social decision making to field data from groups of hundreds of animals. I also develop and teach data science courses for professional audiences.

Organizations I have worked with include Consumer Reports, Workday, Dominion Energy, Seeq, FullContact, and the Allen Institute’s Mindscope Program, along with learners at government agencies and private banks. I teach data science for Data Society, and was a founding instructor of CU Boulder Tech Frontiers.

How people decide under uncertainty

We fit models of how people manage the tradeoff between speed and accuracy, in environments that are complex and that change while a decision is being made. The same models tell us when further data collection has reached diminishing returns, and when a belief has become fixed enough that new evidence won’t change it.

How groups decide

Models of agents deciding together, usually without a central coordinator, and of how the strength of hierarchy changes how well a group performs.

Institutional design

Allocation rules decide what the units under them optimize for. Several years advising university administration on budget and space allocation, including the College of Arts and Sciences budget model and the revenue-linked university-wide budget model.

Forecasting and prediction markets

Polling, election outcomes, and market-implied probabilities are all problems of aggregating noisy evidence about a state that is itself moving. I build Markov and stochastic voting models for these, with attention to how fast a forecast should update as conditions change, and to what the data cannot identify.

Workshops and short courses

Tailored instruction for technical and mixed-background teams, from data science foundations to probabilistic thinking and scientific computing.

Consulting inquiries

I take on a small number of engagements each year, and do not take general data analysis, dashboard or reporting work, or introductory training that an in-house team could run.

Most work is scoped as a project rather than by the hour: a defined question, several weeks, and a written report or a model your team can run. The smallest useful project is a focused model review of around thirty hours. Workshops are priced by the day. I quote once I understand what the question involves, and I am currently booking engagements that begin in January.
