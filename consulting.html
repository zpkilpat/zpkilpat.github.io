Consulting & professional education

Mathematical modeling, decision science, and data science instruction for research groups, companies, and public-sector organizations.

AREAS OF EXPERTISE

Modeling, inference, and instruction

My consulting draws on our group’s research in Bayesian decision making, nonlinear dynamics, and stochastic processes. The models we build are mechanistic rather than black-box: their parameters stand for processes the system actually carries out, so a fit tells you why the data look the way they do, not only what comes next. We have fit Bayesian and heuristic models of human decision making to cohorts of hundreds of subjects, and decentralized models of social decision making to field data from groups of hundreds of animals. I also develop and teach data science courses for professional audiences.

Organizations I have worked with include Consumer Reports, Workday, Dominion Energy, Seeq, FullContact, and the Allen Institute’s Mindscope Program, along with learners at government agencies and private banks. I teach data science for Data Society, and was a founding instructor of CU Boulder Tech Frontiers.

Knowing when to stop

Interim analysis, when to call a test, when to abandon a line of investigation. Stopping rules under uncertainty are the subject of our research, and stopping too early or too late is usually where the money goes.

Decisions when the world keeps changing

Almost every standard method assumes a stationary environment, and almost no real one is. We build and fit models in which the thing being estimated moves, and old evidence has to be discounted at the right rate.

What rule are people actually following?

Not a prediction of what a population will do next, but a model of the decision process generating the behavior, fit to data and validated against it — so the parameters mean something you can act on.

Decisions without a coordinator

How a group of agents reaches, or fails to reach, a collective decision when no one is in charge: how information spreads, how individual differences help, and when coupling becomes counterproductive.

Workshops and short courses

Tailored instruction for technical and mixed-background teams, from data science foundations to probabilistic thinking and scientific computing.

Engagements

Work can take the form of a focused model review, a scoped modeling and data-analysis project, or a customized workshop. Potential topics include sequential decision making, predictive models for dynamical systems, fitting models to behavioral or observational data, and Bayesian methods.

Instruction can be designed for technical staff or broader professional audiences. I take on a small number of engagements each year, and do not take general data analysis, dashboard or reporting work, or introductory training that an in-house team could run.

Advisory work is billed at $250 per hour. Projects typically start at $7,500 — a focused model review is around thirty hours and ends in a written report — and larger engagements are quoted around the question, the preparation involved, and the deliverables.

I am glad to start with a free 20–30 minute call to work out whether I am the right person for the problem.

Institutional design

Separately from the modeling work, I have worked on institutional design inside the university. I helped design the budget model for the College of Arts and Sciences at CU Boulder, and in 2026 served on a university task force developing recommendations on building and infrastructure space under CU Boulder’s financial stewardship initiative.

Allocation rules are mechanism design: they decide what the units under them optimize for, and their second-order effects arrive slowly and are expensive to reverse once everyone has adapted to them. That is recognizably the same problem as the ones above, with departments in place of foragers.

I take on a small number of engagements of this kind each year.
